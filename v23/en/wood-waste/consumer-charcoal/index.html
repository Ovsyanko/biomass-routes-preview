Charcoal briquettes and charcoal for the consumer market

How to turn any type of wood residue into a charcoal briquette–a standardised product for a growing market.

Photo: древесноугольные брикеты Pini Kay / project materials

A charcoal briquette converts sawdust and other fine wood residues into a standardised product for barbecue, grilling, restaurants and retail. One of the most practical routes is to make a dense Pini-Kay or RUF fuel briquette first and then carbonise it under controlled conditions. The result is a high-quality, dense charcoal product that can be made from almost any form of wood residue.

Operating conditions depend on feedstock, briquette geometry and customer specification. Pyrolysis gas should be integrated into the plant’s heat balance where practical.

Why the format interests residue owners

Standardising feedstock before pyrolysis

Pre-drying and briquetting make it possible to produce charcoal not only from specially prepared solid wood, but from sawdust, chips, slabs and wood biomass in other forms.

Higher added value

Charcoal production can convert sawmill residues into a product with higher added value than conventional pellets or wood-fuel briquettes.

The market for charcoal and charcoal briquettes is stable and continues to grow worldwide.

Flexible sales channels

Sales can be organised through several channels:

  • distribution partners;
  • B2B supply to restaurants;
  • own retail sales;
  • marketplaces;
  • contract manufacturing for retail chains;
  • export.
Verifiable engineering concept

Every key stage in the production cycle–feedstock preparation, briquetting and carbonisation (pyrolysis)–is an established operation. Investment risk therefore lies in selecting the right equipment and contractor, using sound calculations and managing the project well. Sales can be secured before plant construction is complete.

Market growth exists, but sales must be designed in advance

Public estimates define the market differently, yet consistently show growing global demand for charcoal briquettes and barbecue products. Producers can address both domestic demand and markets abroad. The global trend alone does not guarantee sales: before investing, it is advisable to select target market segments, agree packaging requirements and obtain preliminary commitments from buyers.

Checks before selecting equipment

  1. Residue volume, moisture, particle size, ash and seasonality.
  2. The full mass and heat balance, including drying and the use of pyrolysis gas.
  3. The required briquette geometry: Pini-Kay, RUF or Nestro.
  4. Finished-charcoal targets: fixed carbon, ash, volatiles, moisture, strength, odour and burn time.
  5. Target sales channel, packaging format and delivered logistics.
