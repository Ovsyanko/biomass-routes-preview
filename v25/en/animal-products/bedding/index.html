Biomass bedding and litter

From local sawdust and straw to purpose-made pellets for pets, poultry farms and stables.

Проектная фотореалистичная иллюстрация

Bedding is not a secondary consumable but an important part of the animal’s environment. It must absorb moisture, maintain a dry and safe surface, minimise dust and odour spread, and provide insulation. Used bedding should be easy to remove and suitable for rational recovery through composting, processing or combustion.

Materials used for bedding include various types of low-value biomass that are sufficiently dry, soft, loose and absorbent. Locally available straw or wood shavings and sawdust are used most often.

2. Loose dry biomass

Sawdust, shavings and straw avoid pelleting cost and can be economical close to a reliable source. Their low bulk density makes long-distance haulage and storage expensive, however, and quality may vary by season and supplier. Fine dust, sharp chips, wet material, mould and contaminated wood are unacceptable.

Marketable straw cannot be inferred from grain yield alone. Cultivar, stem height, weather and harvest method all matter. Short and semi-dwarf cereals may provide less recoverable straw per tonne of grain, while growers may choose to chop and incorporate straw to return organic matter and nutrients to the soil. A feedstock survey must therefore measure the volume farms are actually willing to sell.

3. Pelleted bedding

Pellets cost more to manufacture but reduce storage and freight volume, meter consistently and provide repeatable quality. Bedding pellets do not need maximum fuel-grade durability or density: they should survive logistics and then break down readily to expose an absorbent surface. Diameters of 8 mm and, for some users, 10–12 mm can be practical. Safe mineral sorbents or odour-control additives may be used only after species-specific safety, dust and manure-disposal checks.

4. Specification and broiler-house control

A buyer specification should define initial moisture, absorption and drying methods, particle-size distribution, fines after transport, absence of sharp contaminants, friability, microbiological and chemical safety, and end-of-life requirements.

For broilers, regulation and welfare guidance focus on a continuously dry, friable surface. About 25% litter moisture is a useful management target; at 35–45% litter becomes too wet for birds to work effectively, and zones at 40% or more are associated with cake. Thus, 40% may be used as a ceiling in some specifications, but it is not a desirable operating target. Sample several representative locations, especially around drinkers, feeders and fans.

Moisture is not the only indicator. EFSA recommends ammonia below 15 ppm, while the EU Directive sets 20 ppm at bird-head level for higher-density houses. Foot-pad dermatitis, caking, leaks, dust, relative humidity and ventilation must also be monitored.

5. Market potential

Companion animals

Limited volume but retail prices per kilogram can be several times fuel-pellet prices. Packaging, branding and distribution absorb much of that premium.

Poultry farms

Potentially the largest B2B segment. A premium product must prove lower usage, better logistics and handling, and welfare or production benefits in an on-farm trial.

Stables

A mature professional market in the EU and Gulf states values cleanliness, compact freight and manure management. Russian equestrian centres offer a smaller but credible opportunity.

Rabbits and other animals

Require segment-specific formulations. Low dust, softness and ingestion safety are critical; bedding must not replace forage.

Scale-up should follow a measured customer trial and whole-cycle economics covering production, packaging, freight, usage, labour, animal outcomes and disposal.

Conclusion

Loose bedding is strongest near a consistent local source. Pelleting becomes attractive when biomass must be transported, stored and dosed, and when uniform quality has measurable value. The commercial opportunity lies in a family of purpose-built products rather than one universal pellet.
