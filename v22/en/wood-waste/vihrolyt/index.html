Vihrolyt: a plant–mineral composite

How to convert wood and plant residues into a family of building materials

Illustration: древесностружечная структура, D-Kuru · CC BY-SA 3.0 AT (cropped)

Vihrolyt is a promising family of composite materials in which prepared plant biomass works together with a silicate mineral binder and special additives. It is not one universal material, but a complete platform for boards, panels and moulded elements offering different combinations of weight, strength, thermal and acoustic insulation, and fire-protection properties.

Technology status. The properties described are development targets, supported theoretically and partially confirmed in practice. A test programme will be conducted in 2026 to verify material performance and refine the formulations of the core Vihrolyt series. The result will be an approved product range ready for certification under applicable standards.

1. From waste to high-value building materials

Sawdust, chips, shavings, processed crop residues and other local biomass streams can become composite filler. The objective is not simply to “glue waste together”, but to create a controlled plant–mineral structure and a stable product.

2. Why the construction industry may benefit

A multi-functional material

The goal is to combine structural contribution with thermal and acoustic insulation, reducing the number of layers in the building envelope.

Mineral matrix

It offers potential for improved fire and biological resistance compared with organic-bonded boards; results depend on composition and require testing.

Local feedstock

Low-value wood and plant biomass gains a route into a durable, higher-value product.

Adjustable architecture

Filler fraction, density and product design can be adapted for panels, linings, framed infill and special elements.

4. Waste-utilisation economics

For a residue owner, Vihrolyt is a promising high-value route that can process both large and comparatively small waste streams and is not overly demanding about the characteristics of the residues themselves. The product range can be adapted flexibly to the needs of a local construction market. The most promising chain is “local feedstock – standardised preparation – regional construction market”.
