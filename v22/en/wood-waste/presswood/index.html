Presswood moulded pallets

Product, process, cost and worldwide market logic–with the limits of a working PFS made explicit.

Визуализация: проект Presswood

A Presswood pallet is a one-piece load platform moulded from prepared wood particles and a thermosetting binder. A metered charge is cured in a heated mould, forming the deck, feet and reinforcing geometry in one cycle. The result is not merely compacted waste, but a repeatable logistics product with controlled dimensions, mass and performance.

Data status. The technology and economics are based on calculations for a specific representative facility. They are intended for preliminary assessment and may require adjustment when projects are implemented under different conditions. Although the priority market segments are clear, we recommend confirming sales through commercial contracts or letters of intent before a project is launched. Market references are current to September 2026.

1. Concept and customer advantages

The product family includes 1200×800 and 1200×1000 mm pallets in light and reinforced variants. Mould changes can alter dimensions, rib pattern, feet, mass, branding and target duty. Each design remains a separate product: load rating, deflection, racking behaviour and moisture resistance are confirmed by testing.

Nestability

Empty pallets fit into one another, delivering direct savings in storage space and the cost of shipping or returning empty pallets.

No phytosanitary risk

Moulded processed-wood pallets are accepted for international shipment without fumigation or phytosanitary certificates. Destination-country requirements must still be checked before supply begins.

Uniform yet configurable form

Precise dimensions, a smooth surface, colour and branded marks give high-value shipped goods a consistent professional appearance. The design can be adapted to the load and customer equipment.

One-piece construction

The deck, feet and ribs are formed in one cycle. No pallet assembly or installation of boards, blocks and nails is required.

Competitive cost

At serial production scale, Presswood can cost less than new high-grade wooden pallets. Comparisons must use equivalent load ratings and delivery terms.

Cleanliness and repeatability

No nails, bark or protruding elements. Stable dimensions and mass simplify quality control, stacking and automated logistics.

This is a generic process route. Line configuration, parallel presses and operating conditions depend on the feedstock, product and capacity.

Critical process parameters

  • moisture and particle-size distribution after preparation;
  • feedstock cleanliness, including metals, mineral matter, coatings and treated wood;
  • uniform metering and mixing of an MDI-type binder;
  • charge mass, mould temperature, pressure and cure time;
  • density distribution, dimensional tolerance and batch repeatability;
  • dust extraction, fire and explosion protection, and closed binder handling.

Binder dosage and pressing conditions must be set through laboratory and industrial trials for the chosen feedstock, moisture resistance and mechanical specification.

Calculated EBITDA is RUB 47.5 million. Depreciation, financing, VAT and delivery to the customer are excluded from cash OPEX.

5. Worldwide market and price logic

Moulded pallets do not have a dedicated customs code. HS 441520 combines wooden pallets, box pallets and load boards; it is an adjacent-market proxy, not the size of the Presswood niche. Global imports under this broad code exceeded US$3.66 billion in 2023. This shows the scale of traded pallet demand, but it cannot replace account-level market validation.

The PFS price range of RUB 500–600 excluding VAT is a Russian project case, not a global benchmark. A defensible selling price is the lower of customer value created and the delivered price of the next-best qualified pallet. It must include freight, duty, taxes, distributor margin and any tooling amortisation.

How to size obtainable demand

Build the market from the bottom up: named accounts × annual pallet use × share of flows where the product passes qualification × realistic supplier share. For the PFS line at 294,000 units per year, an investment threshold is to secure preliminary commitments for 40–60% of capacity–about 118,000–177,000 pallets per year–and cover the remaining sales funnel with completed pilots. Top-down market reports cannot substitute for this.

6. Presswood as a wood-waste route

AdvantagesLimitations
  • converts wood residues into a high-value B2B product;
  • can use recovered wood;
  • uses conventional preparation and drying technologies, with wood residues as process fuel;
  • addresses a potentially broad range of customers;
  • creates a standard product with repeat demand;
  • requires comparatively modest investment and can achieve a reasonable 1.5–2.5-year payback, depending on project conditions.
  • economics depend on the cost of imported binder;
  • economics depend on line utilisation;
  • each new pallet variant requires tooling and testing and therefore a sufficiently large minimum production run;
  • a bonded product cannot automatically be considered compostable or fully recyclable.

The route is strongest where clean residues are steady, low-cost drying heat is available and anchor customers are identified before investment. Contaminated or seasonal feedstock and an uncommitted market can make simpler routes–fuel, panels or specialist recycling–more robust.

7. What must be confirmed before the project starts?

  1. Analyse feedstock volume and characteristics across seasons: volume, moisture, particle size, bulk density and contamination.
  2. Order samples of every SKU and test them to the current ISO 8611 series for nominal load, working load, strength and durability, obtaining an official test document.
  3. Obtain a written destination-country position on phytosanitary rules. Processed wood made with glue, heat and pressure is commonly exempt from ISPM 15, but this does not replace verification of importer requirements.
  4. Negotiate with buyers and secure letters of intent or framework agreements for at least 40–60% of project capacity.
  5. Confirm binder price, tool life, mass and heat balances, CAPEX and guaranteed throughput.
  6. Obtain the complete equipment specification and draft supply contracts.
