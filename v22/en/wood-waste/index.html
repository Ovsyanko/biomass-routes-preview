Wood-waste utilization

Routes for sawdust, chips, bark, shavings, offcuts, dust and other forest-industry residues.

Photo: Famartin / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0 · cropped
15 technologies and routes
01

Biomass combustion

Boilers and CHP plants convert prepared biomass into heat and electricity. Key operations include sizing, drying, storage, fuel handling, combustion, ash removal and flue-gas cleaning.

Read article
02

Pellets & fuel briquettes

Densifying milled dry biomass improves transport, storage and combustion consistency. For agricultural residues, ash, slagging and ash chemistry are especially important.

Read article
03

Charcoal briquettes and charcoal for the consumer market

Wood pyrolysis produces lump charcoal; fines can be milled, blended with binder and briquetted. Fixed carbon, ash, volatiles and strength define product quality.

Read article Use in biogas production
04

Hookah charcoal

High-carbon powder is formed into dense briquettes with controlled geometry, burn time, odour, ash and resistance to cracking.

Read article
05

Industrial biocarbon

Biocarbon can partly replace fossil carbon in metallurgy, reduction processes and specialty materials. Specifications include reactivity, strength and impurities.

Read article Use in biogas production
06

Activated carbon

Carbonisation followed by steam or chemical activation creates a highly porous adsorbent for water, air and process-stream purification.

Read article
07

Arbolit, wood wool board & CBPB

Building blocks and boards made with wood filler and a mineral matrix.

Read article
08

Vihrolyt

A family of structural-insulating materials based on plant biomass and a mineral binder. Target properties include fire, biological and moisture resistance with a natural texture.

Read article
09

Wood–plastic composites

Wood flour or fibre is blended with thermoplastic and additives, then extruded or moulded into profiles and products.

Read article
10

Presswood moulded pallets

Wood residues are milled, dried, blended with a binder and moulded into strong, nestable pallets of controlled geometry.

Read article
11

Compost, growing media and organic fertilisers

Controlled aerobic decomposition and stabilisation of clean wood residues.

Learn more
12

Biochar in agriculture

Pyrolysis produces a persistent carbon material. Agronomic effects depend on feedstock, process conditions, dosage and soil; contaminants must be controlled.

Read article Use in biogas production
13

Bedding and litter

Dry, size-reduced biomass—sawdust, shavings, straw and pellets made from them—is used as bedding for companion and farm animals. Absorbency, dust, safety, odour capture and ease of end-of-life handling are key.

Read article
14

Pulp, paper and board

A significant share of sawmill residues and low-grade small-diameter wood is used by the pulp and paper industry as pulp chips. Where pulp chips can be sold for paper production, using this material for other purposes is generally impractical.

Limitations:

  • Limited development of the pulp and paper industry east of the Urals
  • Strict quality requirements for chip cleanliness and dimensions
Learn more
15

Extractives & biochemicals

Bark, wood and agricultural residues can yield tannins, resins, sugars, lignin and other fractions. Cascading use adds value by also utilising the residue.
